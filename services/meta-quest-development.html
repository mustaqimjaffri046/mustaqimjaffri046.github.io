Mixed reality on Meta Quest

Quest headsets can show the real room through their passthrough cameras and place virtual content inside it. Mixed reality suits apps that need the real environment — previewing furniture or interiors in an actual room, guidance next to real equipment, or training that mixes physical and virtual objects.

Meta's mixed reality features include passthrough, scene understanding of walls and furniture, and spatial anchors that keep content in place between sessions. Tell me which of these your app needs, and I'll confirm the approach and the Quest models it requires during scoping.

Testing and release on Quest

  • Builds are profiled and tested on the headset itself, because the editor hides most standalone performance problems.
  • Comfort is part of testing: stable frame rate, locomotion options and readable UI at headset resolution.
  • Release options — a Meta Horizon Store submission, a private release to invited users, or sideloaded builds for your own devices — are chosen during scoping.

Meta Quest FAQ

Which Quest headsets do your apps run on?

My delivered projects run standalone on Oculus Quest 2. Building on OpenXR keeps an app portable to other OpenXR headsets, including PC VR.

OpenXR or the Oculus SDK?

I've used both. OpenXR keeps the app portable across headsets; the Oculus SDK is useful when you need a Quest-specific feature. We choose based on where the app has to run.

Can you turn our Revit or CAD model into a Quest walkthrough?

Yes. I've brought Revit architectural models into Unity and set them up to run on Oculus Quest 2 so people can explore a space at real scale.

Can the app be multi-user?

Yes, with Photon PUN 2 or Unity Netcode and Relay.
