Who VR training simulation is for

Training, operations and L&D teams whose staff need hands-on practice that is expensive, slow or risky to arrange for real — for example in aviation ground handling, logistics and warehousing, workplace safety, transport and manufacturing. Also simulation studios that need an extra Unity XR developer on a training project.

What a VR trainer can include

Guided procedures

A step-by-step task flow that tells trainees what to do next and checks each step before moving on.

Natural input

Hand-gesture recognition, controller interaction and body tracking, depending on what the task needs.

Real-time feedback

Visual cues the moment a signal, step or posture is wrong, so trainees correct it while they practise.

Scoring and session reports

Task scoring and per-session summaries that show how a trainee performed.

Multi-user sessions

Several trainees in one scene, built with Photon PUN 2 or Unity Netcode and Relay.

Runs on standalone headsets

Scenes optimized for Meta Quest, so training doesn't depend on a gaming PC.

How I approach a training simulation

  1. 1

    Map the procedure

    Break the real task into steps with clear pass and fail rules, together with your subject experts.

  2. 2

    Prototype the core interaction

    Build the hardest interaction first — a gesture, a tool, a vehicle control — on the target headset.

  3. 3

    Add scenarios and scoring

    Build the guided flow, feedback and scoring around that interaction, milestone by milestone.

  4. 4

    Optimize on device

    Profile and tune on the headset so the trainer runs smoothly before delivery.

Testing and deployment

  • Builds are tested on the real headset from the first milestone, not only in the Unity editor.
  • Each step's pass and fail rule is checked with your subject experts before scoring is finalized.
  • How the trainer reaches your headsets — a store release, a private release or sideloaded builds for a managed set of devices — is agreed during scoping.
  • Where session results go — an on-device report, an export, or your own system — is defined up front.

What to send for a quote

  • The procedure to train — documents, SOPs or videos of the real task
  • Target headsets and where training will take place
  • Single trainee or several trainees in one session
  • Existing 3D models, CAD files or environments
  • How results should be scored, recorded or reported
  • Your timeline and budget range

VR training FAQ

Which industries have you built VR training for?

Aviation ground handling (aircraft marshalling), warehouse logistics, workplace posture safety and driver training. The same structure — steps, validation, feedback and scoring — carries over to other hands-on procedures.

Which headsets can the trainer run on?

Meta Quest as a standalone headset, and PC VR through OpenXR and SteamVR.

Can several trainees train together?

Yes. Multi-user sessions can be built with Photon PUN 2 or Unity Netcode and Relay, so trainees share one scene in real time.

Can trainee results be recorded?

The posture trainer I built produces a per-session report. How results are stored or sent to your own systems depends on your setup, and we define it when scoping the project.

Planning a VR training simulation?

Describe the procedure you want to train and your target headset. I'll reply with questions, an approach and a quote.
